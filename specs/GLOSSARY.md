# Glossary

The words this project has settled on, what each one means, and what it was
chosen over. Read it before naming a function, a wire tool, a field or a
user-facing string, and add to it whenever a naming question is settled by
argument rather than by accident.

It exists because the cheap way to settle a naming split, counting both
spellings and converging on the bigger pile, gets the answer wrong often enough
to be dangerous. A majority cannot tell a word that won on merit from a word
that won by being written first, and it cannot tell a migration in progress from
drift. Both failures have happened here, and both are recorded below.

## How to read an entry

**A glossary entry outranks a count.** Where this file names a preferred word,
that is the answer regardless of which spelling is currently more common in the
tree. The `audit-hygiene` skill's convention tallies are for finding splits this
file has not yet ruled on; they do not overturn one it has.

**Scope is part of the entry.** A word can be right in one layer and wrong in
the next, and an entry that does not say where it applies will be
over-applied. Each entry below names its scope.

**Direction of travel is part of the entry.** Some entries describe a convention
being migrated to, where the old form is still the majority. New prose follows
the entry, and a file converges as its paragraphs are rewritten for other
reasons. Nobody is expected to sweep the tree, and a one-line edit to a long
file is not an obligation to convert the rest of it.

## The bench

The vocabulary of `crates/sfmtool-core/src/bench/`, the viewer's bench layers
and the wire's bench tools.

| Word | Means | Not | Why |
|------|-------|-----|-----|
| **patch** | the oriented square standing in the world, carrying the content the photographs see there | `surfel`, `frame` | the sense the PatchMatch family uses. Where the geometry alone is meant, without the content, the word is **patch frame**, and bare `frame` names neither |
| **patch frame** | a patch's geometry: its centre and its two half-extent vectors `u` and `v`, which carry its orientation and half-size together; its outward normal is the direction of `u × v`. An `.sfmr` stores the two vectors (`patch_u_halfvec_xyz`, `patch_v_halfvec_xyz`) and takes the centre from the point's own position; core's `OrientedPatch` holds the same frame as `center`, unit `u_axis` and `v_axis` and `half_extent`, with the point's `w` (a bearing's centre is its direction). Identifiers say `patch_frame` (`TrackPayload::patch_frame`, `PointView::patch_frame`, `render_patch_frame`, `NoPatchFrame`), and a type that needs the word says `PatchFrame` (`GridPlanePatchFrame`) | `placement`, bare `frame` | it is already the format's word ([sfmr-file-format.md](formats/sfmr-file-format.md#per-point-patch-frame-optional-version-3), `has_patch_frames`, `rescale_patch_frame`) and the viewer's user-facing one, and the compound collides with none of the other senses of `frame` here: a video or rig frame, a UI frame, a coordinate frame. Bare `frame` suggests the picture frame the patch's content sits in, so it is never the short form, in an identifier, a parameter, a local, a doc comment or a spec. *Placement* was tried and dropped: it reads as the act of putting something somewhere, which is how the tree already uses it for windows, cameras and sightings placed by hand |
| **patch coordinate system** | the patch's centre and its unit axes `u`, `v` and `n = u × v`, with no extent: the coordinate system Set to Origin and Align Normal to Z re-express the scene in. The viewer's `PatchCoordinateSystem` (`display_transform.rs`) holds it as the patch is drawn, in world coordinates | `patch axes`, `PatchAxes`, bare `frame` | a **patch frame** carries the half-extent, the patch's size, along its two vectors; this drops the size and keeps only the origin and the directions, which is what a coordinate system is, so it takes that name rather than a second sense of *frame* |
| **track** | the set of observations across images that are views of one point | | |
| **sighting** | one image's view of the patch, interchangeable with **observation** | | |
| **keypoint** | where one photograph sees the patch's centre | | not the projection of the centre. A gap between the two means that photograph sees the patch **slid** across its line of sight until its centre sits on the keypoint's ray, at `q_i`, where that ray meets the plane through the centre facing that camera (`OrientedPatch::anchored_at_keypoint`; `seen_at_keypoint` for the tile and the drawn outline, falling back to the unslid patch where the ray is at or past a right angle to the line of sight, where the hand edits refuse). The slid patch keeps the patch's axes and size, a parallel copy off its plane. The slid patch is the square the photograph shows: the viewer draws it, a pointer on it is read against it, and the view's tile is cut through it. A tilt, a spin and a resize about the centre keep every keypoint; a translation by `d`, and a resize from an edge, carry each slid point, the keypoint becoming `P_i(q_i')`, the slid centre carried with the patch (`OrientedPatch::carried_slide_center`) |
| **bench** | where an item is held and judged before it is committed | | |
| **focused item** | the one item Track View edits while its *Edit* box is ticked, and the item a bench tool acts on when it names none: at most one for the whole viewer, named by a node and an item on that node's bench (`AppState::focused_item`, a `FocusedItem`), whether the item is at the cluster or the track stage, and possibly none. Held beside the selection, not in a version. Wire: `get_bench`'s `focused_item` | `active item`, `the focus`, `edit focus`, `current item` | *focus* is only ever a verb, so the state it produces is the *focused item* and never "the focus". A Scene tree row is *selected* and a dock tab is *raised*, so bench prose that says "focused" means this and nothing else |
| **focus** / **unfocus** | make an item the focused item / leave no item focused, every item staying on its bench. Neither is a step: no version, one `Selection` row (`Editing X`, `Stopped editing X`). `AppState::focus_bench_item`, `AppState::unfocus_bench_item`; wire `focus_bench_item`, `unfocus_bench_item` | `activate` / `deactivate`, `clear`, `unset`, `close` | the pair names what it acts on, so `unfocus_bench_item` sits beside `focus_bench_item` and needs no argument, there being one focused item for the viewer; *discard* is the separate step that takes an item off the bench. The Action Log says *Editing*, the word of the *Edit* box a person sees |
| **viewed track** | the selected point read as an editable track and held off every bench: core's `create_track` applied to the point in the version at the cursor, labelled with its portable ID, evaluated live as a bench track is, and drawn by Track View in Viewed mode. `ViewedTrack`, held in `AppState::viewed_tracks` (a cache of the last few, keyed by node, point and document serial) and read through `AppState::viewed_track`; the wire reads its evaluation as `get_point`'s `evaluation` block | `preview track`, `committed track`, `view-mode track` | *viewed* says what it is for, being looked at, and matches the mode that draws it. *Preview* is taken by a drag's transient track, and *committed track* names the point's own record, which this is a reading of and not the same value: it carries bench measurements the point does not |
| **recent items** | the items focused this session, most recently focused first and each once: `AppState::recent_items`, a list of `FocusedItem`, moved to the front on every focus and pruned when a node closes. Track View's **recent items strip** draws the first eight on a bench at the cursor, leaving out the focused item, as chips to the right of the *Edit* box (`track_view::recent`, `RecentStrip`), and a tick of *Edit* with no point selected focuses the first (`AppState::most_recent_item`) | `history`, `recently edited`, `recent tracks` | *history* is the version list's word (the Edit History panel), and this list is not in a version. *Items* rather than *tracks* because a cluster is on it too. *Recent* rather than *recently edited* because what puts an item on it is a focus, which may involve no edit at all |
| **Lock** | the checkbox in Track View's Edited-mode toolbar saying what Image Detail's dot does at the track stage: ticked, it slides the patch and every sighting follows; cleared, it moves that one sighting's keypoint. A tool setting, never bench state, so it costs no version; Surface Tiling Mode holds it ticked while the mode is on; field and accessor `lock` | `pin`, `couple`, `link` | `pin` is taken: a pinned observation is one whose verdict was set by hand, and an unlocked move pins as well, so the word cannot also name the setting. It is the user's own label for the box. Distinct from the **Move Camera lock** of camera view, which is viewport state of another panel; bench prose says *Lock* in italics or "Track View's Lock" where the two could be confused |
| **walk** / **Accept walk** | the move a fit's kernels would make to a track-stage sighting; one further than the track's `max_shift_px` is refused and the sighting kept at its seed, with `walked_px` (how far), `walked_to` (the pixel) and `walked_plain_zncc` and `walked_blur_matched_zncc` (the plain and blur-matched ZNCC against the stored bitmap of the tile there) on its `TrackMeasurement`. *Accept walk* is Track View's row entry that overrules the refusal by putting the sighting at `walked_to` with `sight_observation`; `AppState::accept_bench_walk`, and on the wire `sight_bench_observation` with that pixel | `drift`, `jump`, `snap` | *walk* was already the Status cell's and the field's word (`walked 19 px, kept at seed`, `walked_px`), so the new fields and the entry extend it rather than naming the same event twice. *Drift* is the cluster refinement's word for a member's move from its seed, which is a measurement and not a refused move |
| **no reference** (an unmeasured bench row) | the reason a fit's localizer could not place a row: its round had no reference render to align it to, because no other observation in the round could be read or the reference observation's own tile left its photograph or carried no texture. `Unmeasured::NoReference`, shown as "there is no reference render to align it to" | `no consensus` | the localizer aligns every view to the reference render, so what is missing is the reference; there is no consensus to lack |
| **keep**, **pin** | *Keep* is Track View's switch for a bench observation's verdict: on is `in`, off is `out`. A *pin* says the verdict was set by hand, and the thresholds leave a pinned verdict alone; an unpinned verdict is always the bars' to set, on every evaluation and every threshold painting. The pushpin beside the switch shows it and toggles it, and the one in the *Keep* heading unpins every row while any is pinned and pins every row as it stands while none is. The verdict itself stays `in` / `out` in core, on the wire and in Python, and the pin is never a verdict: the wire's three tools are core's three steps, `set_bench_observation_verdict` setting one observation `in` or `out` by hand, which pins it (core's `set_verdict`, Python's `set_verdict`), `pin_bench_observations` fixing verdicts as they stand (core's `pin_verdicts`, Python's `pin_verdict`), and `unpin_bench_observations` giving verdicts back to the thresholds (core's `unpin_verdicts`, Python's `unpin_verdict`) | `verdict` as the control's name, `candidate` as a third verdict, `pin` / `unpin` as values of a `verdict` argument | *Keep* says what a switch that is on does to the observation, where *verdict* names the kind of value and leaves "on" to be explained. A third state named *candidate* stood for "not ruled on", which the pin already says: an unpinned `out` is exactly an observation nobody ruled on. The wire once had one tool, `set_bench_track_verdict`, whose `verdict` took `pin` and `unpin` beside `in` and `out`; that put the pin among the verdicts and needed a rule spanning two arguments (`observations` only with `pin` or `unpin`), so it was split into the three tools, each named for the observations it acts on as the other `*_bench_observation(s)` tools are |
| **geometry search min relative ZNCC** | the track's bar for *Find matches by geometry*: the fraction of the track's own self-agreement a photograph's ZNCC to the reference has to reach for the search to add it. Field `Thresholds::geometry_search_min_relative_zncc`, the same key on the wire and in Python, and Track View's box *geometry search min relative ZNCC (%)*, which stands above the table rather than in the threshold row. The search it tunes is core's `search_geometry`, wire `search_bench_track_geometry` | `min_relative_zncc` for the track's bar, `matches_search_…` | it is held in `Thresholds` beside the four bars that judge rows, but judges none, so the name says which operation reads it. *Geometry search* is the name core, the wire and the row's menu entry already give that operation; *matches search* would also fit the descriptor search (`search_bench_track_descriptors`, *Find matches by SIFT query*), which does not read it. View selection's and the keypoint localizer's own `min_relative_zncc` parameters keep their name, since in those settings structs there is no other bar to confuse it with |
| **middle ZNCC** | the second reading of the samples a patch ZNCC was computed from: the same two bitmaps, the same peak, correlated over only the centred square half the grid's width (rows and columns `R/4 .. R - R/4`). Field suffix `_middle` on the ZNCC it belongs to (`zncc_middle` at the cluster stage, `plain_zncc_middle`, `blur_matched_zncc_middle`, `walked_plain_zncc_middle` and `walked_blur_matched_zncc_middle` at the track stage, `member_zncc_middle`), and the bar `min_zncc_middle` | `centre ZNCC`, `core ZNCC`, `inner ZNCC` | *centre* names a point, and this reading covers a quarter of the patch's area. *Core* is taken: the localizer's `R×R` core is the whole scored grid. *Inner* suggests a complement called *outer*, and there is none. *Middle* was already the word the track-at-pixel harness and the viewer's request used, and the suffix form keeps each middle reading sorted beside the ZNCC it is read from |
| **ZNCC grid** | the nine readings of the samples a patch ZNCC was computed from over a three-by-three split of the grid's whole square, the window's disk playing no part, rows and columns cut at `R/3` and `R - R/3`, with every pixel weighted equally, held `grid[row][col]` from the top-left cell in the layout the patch tile is drawn in. Field suffix `_grid` on the ZNCC it belongs to (`zncc_grid` at the cluster stage, `plain_zncc_grid`, `blur_matched_zncc_grid`, `walked_plain_zncc_grid` and `walked_blur_matched_zncc_grid` at the track stage, `member_zncc_grid`); a cell of it is a *cell* in identifiers and the core specs (`cell_deficit`, `CellAgreement`, `REFERENCE_MIN_JUDGED_CELL_ZNCC`), and a **ninth** (of the tile or the patch) in what a person reads beside a table: Track View's columns and hover text (`ninth differs`), the bench and GUI specs' prose, and the wire's tool descriptions | `ZNCC cells`, `ZNCC tiles`, `3x3 ZNCC` | *grid* is the word the request used and says the layout: nine readings in the shape of the patch. *Tile* is taken, since it is the rendered patch picture a Track View row draws. The `_grid` suffix matches `_middle`, so the parts' readings sort beside the ZNCC they are read from. *Ninth* is the word in Track View because a *cell* there is already one row's entry in one column, and "hover a cell" would otherwise name two things; it says the same nine-way split in plain words. |
| **ZNCC self-similarity radius** | how far, in patch pixels up to a bound, a patch can shift over itself while its ZNCC with itself stays within the deficit a true match between two views shows: the semi-major axis of the **ellipse** of the shifts where that ZNCC, computed at whole-pixel shifts and interpolated bilinearly between them, is at or above the level, where the bound reads "that far or further". Functions `zncc_self_similarity_radius` (one template) and `zncc_self_similarity_parts` (a bitmap whole, its middle and its cells), bindings `zncc_self_similarity_parts` and `zncc_self_similarity_parts_stack`, fields `zncc_self_similarity_radius` with `_middle` and `_grid` ([core/patch/zncc-self-similarity-radius.md](core/patch/zncc-self-similarity-radius.md)). The bench's bar on the whole tile's radius is `max_zncc_self_similarity_radius` (default `BENCH_MAX_ZNCC_SELF_SIMILARITY_RADIUS`), and Track View's box for it stands in the threshold row under the *Self-similarity* heading, followed by *px whole*. The keypoint localizer's gate on a view's own core is `max_member_zncc_self_similarity_radius` (flag `--max-member-zncc-self-similarity-radius` on `embed-patches`), cluster-patch refinement's gate on a member's own patch has the same name (`ClusterRefineParams::max_member_zncc_self_similarity_radius`, flag `--max-member-zncc-self-similarity-radius` on `cluster-patches` and key of the same name in a `.matches` file's `refine_options`), Add Image to Tracks' keyword for the same gate is `max_zncc_self_similarity_radius`, and the value a search reports is `ViewSearch::zncc_self_similarity_radius` and the per-candidate `zncc_self_similarity_radius`. The cull on a point's stored patch bitmap (the reference view's render) is `max_zncc_self_similarity_radius` in `embed-patches` (flag `--max-zncc-self-similarity-radius`, key of the same name in the written `.sfmr`'s `tool_options`) and `--filter-by-zncc-self-similarity-radius` in `xform` (`FilterByZnccSelfSimilarityRadiusTransform`). The reading of a bitmap as it is, with no pixels from outside it, which at each shift correlates only the samples that have data on both sides, is the **overlap reading**, the only reading the functions make, so their names carry no suffix for it | `autocorrelation radius`, `self-similarity radius`, `lock-in radius`, `autocorrelation percentile pixels` | *ZNCC* names the normalization, since textbook autocorrelation normalizes the whole signal once and this normalizes each window by its own mean and spread. *Self-similarity* names the surface the radius is read from, the patch's ZNCC with itself at each shift. *Lock-in* is taken by Track View's **Lock**, and nothing here is a percentile. The bar is the field it judges with `max_` in front, as `max_shift_px` is. The culls on the stored bitmap drop `member`, since they judge the point's bitmap rather than a member's own tile, so `embed-patches`' cull takes the bench's name for its bar. The localizer's gate carries `member`, which says it judges one view's own tile rather than the point's stored bitmap, as cluster refinement's `member_` fields do, and cluster refinement's gate takes the same name because it is the same gate on the same kind of tile; Add Image to Tracks judges only the one new view, so its keyword drops it and matches the bench's bar. The box needs no visible label of its own, since it stands under the heading of the readings it judges and is followed by the unit and name those readings print with; its name in the accessibility tree and `get_widgets` is `maximum Self-similarity whole (px)` ([gui/track-view.md](gui/track-view.md) § "The thresholds") |
| **far-field sweep** | a pixel's patch read from infinity inwards along its ray, at disparities counted in the image that moves the pixel most, with each peak of the reading returned as a **far-field reading**; `sfmtool_core::bench::far_field_sweep`, `FarFieldReading`, `FarFieldOptions`, harness source name `farfield`. A **disparity** here is always that image's shift in px, `0` being infinity | `infinity test`, `plane sweep`, `far anchor` | the *infinity test* is the harness's older test, which asks only whether the pixel is at infinity; the sweep asks where in the far field it reads. *Plane sweep* is the track-at-pixel sweep member and `candidates/planesweep.py`, which sweep a scene's whole depth range for one best depth. *Anchor* keeps its track-at-pixel meaning, the anchored fit |
| **distance range** / **bounded** / **far** | the distances along a pixel's ray at which every other sighting of a point stays within its tolerance, `[near, far]` with `far` possibly infinite (`sfmtool_core::bench::distance_range`, harness `range`). A range is **bounded** when finite at both ends, above zero, and no wider than `max_span` far over near, and **far** when it has no far end and its near end is at least `far_spread` **camera spreads** out, the camera spread being the largest distance between two camera centres (`camera_spread`); either makes it **usable** (`RangeClass`, `classify_range`) | `depth range`, `depth interval`, `finite`, `at infinity` | *depth* is a distance along a camera's axis (`FarFieldReading::depth`), and the range is measured along the pixel's ray from the camera centre, so the two differ off the axis. *Finite* would include the ranges too wide to use, and a far range is not a claim that the point is at infinity, only that the photographs cannot tell it from there |
| **matching source** / **nearby candidate** | one of the ways the finder of nearby tracks proposes candidate tracks near a pixel, each from its own evidence: the reconstruction's own points (`nearby_points`, `NearbySource::Points`, harness `tracks`) the cluster-patches clusters (`nearby_cluster_tracks`, `Clusters`, harness `clusters`) and **guided matching**, the keypoints near the pixel matched by descriptor among the keypoints whose rays pass close to theirs (`guided_matches`, `Guided`, harness `guided`) and the SIFT index's constellation query from the pixel, its carried positions triangulated (`constellation_seeds`, `Constellation`, harness `constellation`); and the value every source returns, a point with its sightings, the queried image's first, and how well they meet (`NearbyCandidate`) | `anchor`, `hypothesis`, `seed`, `tracks` for the points source | *anchor* keeps its track-at-pixel meaning, the anchored fit, as the far-field sweep row says. *Seed* is the bench's word for where a new observation starts (`ObservationSeed`, `ClusterSeed`), and *hypothesis* says what a candidate is to the comparison that follows rather than what it is. *Candidate* says it is proposed and not yet judged, and *nearby* ties it to the finder's own *nearby track*, which it becomes once judged. The harness calls the points source `tracks`, but every candidate carries a track; what sets that source apart is that its candidates are existing points |
| **depth layer** | the usable nearby candidates whose distance ranges overlap, grouped, with the union of their ranges; the layers of a query are nearest first and do not overlap (`sfmtool_core::bench::depth_layers`, `DepthLayer`, harness `layers`) | `layer` alone, `surface`, `depth hypothesis` | *layer* alone is taken by the viewer's drawing layers (`Layer::ghost`, Image Detail's overlays), so this one is always qualified. It is not a *surface*: two surfaces whose ranges overlap share a layer, and the photographs cannot say which it is. *Depth* is loose here, since the range is along the pixel's ray, but *depth layer* is the name the plan and the harness settled on and a reader hears it right |
| **support** (of a nearby candidate) / **weight** (of a depth layer) | a candidate's support is how many other usable candidates have ranges overlapping its own and images not all among its own, nor its among theirs: independent readings of the same distance (`DepthLayers::support`, harness `support` on an anchor). A layer's **weight** is the sum over its members of `log2(1 + views)` times `exp(-distance_px / 20)` (`LayerEvidence::weight`, harness `support` in a layer's evidence) | `support` for the weight, `corroboration` | the harness used *support* for both, a count on each anchor and a weighted sum on each layer, which read the same in a table and are not. The count keeps the word, since it says what support is, another reading agreeing; the sum is renamed in core and keeps the harness's key in the binding's dicts |
| **score** / **key** / **rank** / **confidence** (of a depth layer) | the **score** is the pixel's own patch read at the layer, `(photo + photo_both) / 2`; the **key** is what the layers are ranked by, the score plus the middle's reading less a little for distance from the pixel; the **rank** is the layer's place by the key, 1 first; the **confidence** is the fitted logistic chance that the pixel is on the layer (`LayerRanking`, harness `score`, `key`, `rank`, `confidence`) | `score` for the key, `probability`, `likelihood` | *score* was the harness's first ranking value and stays the patch reading, so the value that adds the other evidence needs its own word; *key* is the sort key. *Probability* claims a calibration the logistic was only fitted toward. *Confidence* is always of something: `normal_confidence` in a `.sfmr` is how sure a patch normal is, unrelated to this one |
| **nearby track** | one of the tracks found near a pixel of one photograph by the combined operation, with its distance range, class, depth layer and label; for an existing point the point it is, for the rest a track-stage track built from its sightings (`sfmtool_core::bench::find_nearby_tracks`, `NearbyTrack`, `NearbyTracks`; harness `anchor`, `find_anchors`). The viewer's menu entry is *Find Nearby Tracks* | `anchor`, `nearby candidate` for the result | *anchor* keeps its track-at-pixel meaning, the anchored fit that slides a patch onto the pixel, as the far-field sweep row says; the harness's name for these came before that one was settled. A *nearby candidate* is what a matching source proposes; once it has its range and class and is placed in a layer, it is a nearby track, whether or not it is usable |
| **group label** / **track label** (of nearby tracks) | a query's **group label** is the queried image's stem and the rounded pixel, `<stem>@<x>,<y>`, the form a cluster seeded at the pixel takes (`nearby_group_label`); a caller's label replaces it. Each usable nearby track's **track label** is the group label, the layer's rank, a letter for its place in the layer by distance from the pixel (`a` first, `z` then `aa`), and ` pt <index>` for an existing point: `frame_13@412,230 1b pt 812` (`nearby_track_label`) | a running number, the source's name in the label | the rank says which depth the photographs favour, so the labels sort best first and `1a` is the track nearest the pixel on the best-supported layer, the one a caller starts from. The source is in the track, not its name, since two sources can find the same point |
| **evaluation** / **evaluating** (on the bench) | the reading of a bench track at the stage it is in, which moves nothing; in the viewer it is **live**, run on its own after every change to one of its inputs, and its state is `current`, `evaluating`, `refused` or `failed` (`crate::bench::live::Evaluation`, the wire's `evaluation.state`) | `reading` (in names), `refresh`, `auto-evaluate`, `stale` | core already named the step `evaluate` and its types `Evaluate*`, so the state and the wire field extend that word. *Evaluating* names what is happening to the numbers shown, where *stale* would name only that they are old and say nothing about a newer answer being on its way |
| **ghost outline** | Image Detail's outline of the track-stage patch as it stands, projected into an image the track has no observation in and drawn at the ghost opacity (0.8); a handle for the patch-wide edits while Track View's *Lock* is ticked, display only while it is cleared; constant `GHOST_OPACITY`, built by `Layer::ghost` | `phantom`, `preview`, `projection` | *preview* is taken by the drag's transient track and *projection* by the patch's projected centre and the *Proj. err (px / deg)* column. *Ghost* is the usual word in editors for a faint stand-in for the real thing, and the faintness is what stays constant: the ghost keeps its opacity whether or not it takes input, so being editable does not make it read as a sighting |
| **viewpoint** | which photograph a pixel of a gesture is in, and so which square it is read against: an observation's image, the patch slid onto its keypoint's ray (`bench::geometry::SeenSquare` in the viewer), or an image named as such, the patch as it stands; type `sfmtool_core::bench::Viewpoint`, wire spelling `observation` or `camera_image` | `view`, `target`, `source` | `view` is taken by the viewport and Image Detail's pan and zoom, and `target` by the wire's `TranslateTarget` and `ResizeTarget`, which say what a call wants rather than where its pixel was read. A viewpoint is the place a picture is taken from, which is the whole of what the two variants tell apart |

**The four gestures are named for the axis they act on**, so that no two are
synonyms: **translate** moves the centre, **resize** changes the half-length,
**spin** turns the square about its normal, and **tilt** turns the normal
itself. The pairs this replaced were unusable. `translate` and `offset` are
English synonyms that named perpendicular motions, and `rotate` is the generic
word for turning, which cannot stand beside a specific one.

**A wire tool is named for the part it acts on**, not for the item it is
addressed through. `translate_bench_patch` acts on the patch,
`sight_bench_observation` on one sighting, `commit_bench_track` on the track,
and `duplicate_bench_item` on either kind. The name that made this rule
necessary was `move_bench_track_observation`, which moves an observation and not
a track.

**Each word is said once along the path**, the enclosing namespace carrying what
it already states. A core step sits in `sfmtool_core::bench` and takes an
`&EditableTrack`, so it is `tilt_patch`. A viewer edit sits in `PatchEdit`, so
it is `PatchEdit::Tilt`. Only the wire, whose namespace is flat, spells all
three, as `tilt_bench_patch`. A name like `bench_track_frame` is three words
where the context has already supplied two.

|------|-------|-----|-----|
| **sampler** | how one render resamples the photograph's pyramid: `bilinear`, `bilinear_mip` or `anisotropic` (`Sampler`). The kernels' parameter is named `sampler` whether it holds one sampler or the sampler rule (`SamplerChoice`) | `filter`, `interpolation` | names the whole of what a render does to the pyramid; a filter is one part of it |
| **sampler rule** | the choice of `anisotropic` or `bilinear_mip` for each observation from its Jacobian: `anisotropic` when `σ_major ≥ √2` and `L ≥ a`. `SamplerChoice::PerView`, spelled `per_view` in the bindings, on the command line and in `tool_options` | `adaptive`, `auto`, `mixed` sampler | a rule names what decides; `adaptive` and `auto` say only that something does, and `mixed` describes a stack rather than one view. `per_view` says which unit the rule decides for, beside the three samplers it chooses among |
| **minor axis loss** (`L`) | how many times coarser `bilinear_mip` reads a view's less compressed axis than that axis's own compression needs, `2^l / max(σ_minor, 1)` with `l = round(log2 max(σ_major, 1))`, the level it reads before any clamp to the pyramid's depth; `minor_axis_loss`, `sampler_minor_axis_loss` on the wire, and the loss Track View names on a *Zoom* cell's hover | `anisotropy`, `aniso ratio` | it is the quantity the sampler rule compares with `a`, and it is not the ratio of the singular values: a view compressed alike on both axes reads an `L` up to √2 from the level rounding alone |
| **anisotropic threshold** (`a`) | the bar on `L` at which the rule moves a view, `anisotropic_threshold` in `SamplerChoice::PerView`, `DEFAULT_ANISOTROPIC_THRESHOLD` and `tool_options` | `aniso_ratio`, `switch level` | it is compared with `L`, the minor axis's loss, not with the ratio of the singular values, so a name with *ratio* in it would point at the wrong number |
| **moved** (a view) | a view the sampler rule renders with `anisotropic`; one it leaves on `bilinear_mip` is **unmoved** | `upgraded`, `switched` | the word the draft and the measurements use for the views whose tile changes; *upgraded* would claim the change is always an improvement, which the ZNCC against the other views does not bear out everywhere |

Suffixes of Rust struct names across the Cargo workspace. The bindings and

|------|-------|-----|-----|

## Cluster-patch refinement

The words of `patch::cluster_refine`'s cascade starts and piecewise stage,
the `.matches` per-cell entries, the cell plane normals and the specs that
describe them
([core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md](core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md)).

| Word | Means | Not | Why |
|------|-------|-----|-----|
| **piecewise refinement** | the optional stage after the affine cascade that registers each kept member's nine cells separately against the member's photograph, starting at the cascade's shape, and stores where each cell's content lies. `ClusterRefineParams::piecewise`, `PiecewiseParams`, flag `--piecewise` on `cluster-patches`, keyword `piecewise=` and key `piecewise` in `refine_options`. By default it also moves the shape, by the loop `move_shape` turns on; with `move_shape` off it only measures | `cell refinement`, `per-cell fit`, `piece-gated refinement` | *piecewise* is the name the flag, the type and the stored option already carry, and it says the patch is read part by part. It keeps *refinement* because it is a stage of cluster-patch refinement and, by default, refines the shape. *Piece-gated* names the rendering normal estimator of the drafts, a different consumer of the same idea |
| **cell** (of a patch) | one part of the three-by-three split of a patch's `R × R` grid, rows and columns cut at `R/3` and `R − R/3`, `[row][col]` from the top-left: the split the ZNCC grid uses. Identifiers `member_cell_*`, `CellStatus`, `CellRefinement`, `cell_plane_normals`. A cell's **displacement** is where its content lies relative to where the member's stored shape places its centre, with no affine map removed (`member_cell_shift_px`) | `piece`, `ninth`, `tile`; `residual` for the displacement | *piece* is the bench's word for the finite-difference normal's parts, whose count is a parameter and whose neighbours overlap; a cell is always one of a fixed nine that tile the grid exactly. *Ninth* is the person-facing word for the same split in Track View, where *cell* already means a table entry. *Tile* is a rendered picture. The displacement is not a *residual*, because a residual would say an affine map had been removed, and the stored value deliberately keeps it |
| **capped cell** | a cell of a member's own grid whose ZNCC self-similarity radius is the largest the reading reports, `max_radius` (3, "3 or further"), or that has no reading. Cluster-patch refinement's gate on how many of a member's nine cells are capped at its refined shape is `ClusterRefineParams::max_capped_cells` (flag `--max-capped-cells` on `cluster-patches`, key of the same name in `refine_options`), counted by `capped_cell_count`; a member over it is `rejected_unlocalizable_cells`. A member whose whole grid at its refined shape is over the member gate's bar (`regate_at_refined_shape`, flag `--regate-at-refined-shape`) is `rejected_unlocalizable_refined` ([core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md](core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md#the-member-gate-at-the-refined-shape)) | `saturated cell`, `flat cell` | *capped* is the word the ellipse's lengths already use for reaching `max_radius`. *Saturated* would read as a clipped colour, which this glossary calls the **clipped share**, and *flat* is only one way a cell reads the cap, a straight edge being another. The two statuses keep `rejected_unlocalizable` as their stem, since they refuse a member for the reason the up-front gate does, read at another shape |
| **rotation start** | one of the rotations of a member's SIFT seed warp, evenly spaced over a full turn with the seed itself first, at which the reference's template is scored before the affine cascade, which then runs from the best-scoring one. The count is `ClusterRefineParams::rotation_starts` (`DEFAULT_ROTATION_STARTS`, 12), flag `--rotation-starts` on `cluster-patches`, keyword `rotation_starts=` and key `rotation_starts` in `refine_options` ([core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md](core/patch/cluster-patch-refinement.md#rotation-starts)) | `multi-start`, `rotation seed`, `orientation candidate`, `num_rotations` | *start* is what an optimiser starts from, and the count names how many it is offered; *multi-start* names the technique, not the thing counted, so it does not read as a number. *Seed* already means the SIFT seed `M₀` throughout the spec, and a second seed per member would blur which one is the detection's. *Orientation* is SIFT's own word for the angle the seed was built from, the angle the starts exist to second-guess. `num_rotations` would read as how far the warp turns |
| **working patch** / **context tile** | the photograph resampled onto the template's grid plus a margin of `cell_shift_bound_px` on every side, made once per render; the only thing the cell search reads. The *template* is the reference member's samples the cells are cut from. A *bitmap* is a point's stored patch content. *Working* says it exists only for the stage's own search and is never stored |

## Words with a boundary


**patch** (the bench) and **surfel** (the scene renderer). Both name an
`OrientedPatch`, and the split is deliberate rather than settled by a count: the
renderer is doing surfel splatting, which is what that word means in graphics,
while the bench is editing one patch in the PatchMatch sense. `surfel` is
correct in `scene_renderer/`, `track_view/`, `state*` and the specs that
describe rendering. Inside the bench it is wrong. *If this boundary is ever
removed it should be removed deliberately and in one pass, not eroded from
either side.*

**`_name`** (internal) and **`name`** (public). Scope: Python module,
function, class, constant and attribute names under `src/sfmtool/`. A leading
`_` means internal to `sfmtool`, not public API: any code inside the package
may import it, across subpackages, while `scripts/`, the docs, spec examples
and users' code do not, and a test does only when the internal name is what it
tests. A name with no `_`, reached by a path with no `_`-prefixed component, is
public. A re-exporting subpackage (`compare/`, `feature_match/`, `strips/`,
`visualization/`, `xform/`) has `_`-named modules because its public names are
the ones its `__init__.py` re-exports. The full rule is in
[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) § "Python names and privacy".

## Contrast pairs that are not synonyms

Two words that look interchangeable and are not. Using either for the other
loses a distinction the code depends on.

| Pair | The distinction |
|------|-----------------|
| **retriangulate** / **direction re-estimation** | retriangulation settles a finite point from its observations. A direction re-estimation settles the **bearing** of a point at infinity, which has no depth to solve for. The specs name both in one breath, so neither word is available as a loose synonym for the other |
| **`likelihood`** / **`floor`** (rules of the retriangulation operation), and the verdict **`no_depth`** | both decide whether a track becomes a bearing from its own rays. The `floor` is a geometric cut: a track whose widest ray pair is narrower than an angle is `thin`. The `likelihood` rule reads the point-or-bearing likelihood-ratio test at a stated noise level (`LikelihoodRule`, the binding's `likelihood_sigma_px`), and a track it finds no depth in has the verdict `no_depth` (`PointVerdict::NoDepth`, the census's `no_depth`). The rule is named after the statistic it reads, as the `floor` is after its bound; *score* is the depth layers' word, and *point_or_bearing* names the test's two outcomes rather than a rule. The verdict is not *bearing*, because `marked`, `thin`, `behind` and `over_bar` end with a bearing too and a verdict names the rule that decided, and not *thin*, which is the angle's word |
| **reprojection noise** / **noise floor** | the reprojection noise is a measurement: the RMS per-axis reprojection residual over a reconstruction's observations of finite points, with **outliers** left out (`SfmrReconstruction::reprojection_noise_px`, and `reprojection_noise` for the per-camera values and the `outlier_count`). An outlier, here, is an observation whose residual is longer than `OUTLIER_GATE` robust spreads of its camera's residuals: a residual no noise model explains, not the heavy tail the measure is meant to cover, so the measure is not called a robust spread or a trimmed RMS. A noise floor is a policy: a lower bound a caller puts under a per-point noise estimate (`noise_floor_px`, and the `noise_px` arguments that take `max(error, noise_px)`). An argument that takes a noise level to weight rays by, measured or not, is `sigma_px`. *Noise floor* is not a name for the measured value, since the measured value has no floor under it, and the point-or-bearing test reads it with none. The measured level is never under the **keypoint resolution** (`keypoint_resolution_px`), the finest pixel coordinate an `f32` keypoint states (`f32::EPSILON` times the image's larger dimension), so that keypoints at their exact projections, whose residuals are 0, still weight their rays; that bound comes from how a keypoint is stored, not from a policy about noise, and it is not called a noise floor either |
| **promote** / **demote** (a point) | to promote is to store a point at infinity as a finite point (`w = 0` to `w = 1`), and to demote is to store a finite point as a point at infinity, each on a verdict of the point-or-bearing test: `SfmrReconstruction::classify_points_at_infinity` and its `InfinityReclassification` counts (`promoted`, `demoted`), the reports' *promotions* and *demotions*, and `sfm xform --classify-points-at-infinity`'s output. Not *relabel*, the word while the pass only moved points one way and kept what their coordinate meant: a promotion places the point. And *materialise* (`materialize_points_at_infinity`) is not a promotion: it supplies a depth the data does not give, for a consumer that cannot store `w = 0` |
| **storage decision** / **inverse depth** (bundle adjustment's free points) | under `FreePointPolicy::cross` the adjustment solves each free point in inverse depth -- an anchor `a`, a unit direction `u` and `ρ ≥ 0`, the point at `a + u/ρ` -- and the storage decision is the one reading of the point-or-bearing test, at the end of the solve, that says whether the point is stored as a position or as a direction (`FreePointDecision`, the binding's `free_point_decision`). The crossing is the default, `FreePointPolicy::CROSS`; the opt-out, under which each free point keeps the representation it is handed in for the whole solve, is `FreePointPolicy::NO_CROSS`, spelled `cross=off` on `sfm xform --bundle-adjust` and `free_points_cross=False` in the bindings and on the wire. The decision is not called the *crossing*, nor a *reclassification*: the policy keeps the name `cross`, since free points still move between a position and a direction, but they now do so within a round, and *crossing* alone would read as the re-decision at every round boundary that the storage decision replaced. It is not a reclassification, which is a pass over a stored reconstruction with its own counts (`promoted`, `demoted`); the decision counts `to_finite` and `to_direction` against what the caller handed in, and `unscored` for the free points the final round kept too few observations of to score, which are stored in the representation the caller handed them in (or, handed in with no estimate, the one the re-estimation gave them) and counted in neither -- *unscored* rather than *undecided*, since `decided` is the whole decision's flag, and rather than *starved*, which names why a track lost its observations rather than what the decision did with it. The opt-out is `NO_CROSS` because the policy is a struct holding one boolean, `cross`, and `NO_CROSS` is exactly `FreePointPolicy { cross: false }`: the Rust constant uses the same word as the field, as `cross=off` and `free_points_cross=False` do. It is not `KEEP`, the constant's earlier name, because *keep* describes what the solve then does to the representation rather than which switch is set, and because *keep* is already Track View's word for an observation's verdict switch (see **keep**, **pin**); not *hold*, which is a held point's word; and not *off* alone, which names the switch's position without naming the switch |
| **in-front floor** / **`floor`** (the retriangulation rule) | the in-front floor is the bound the bundle adjustment's trim holds an observation's **in-front measure** to (the canonical depth `−z_cam` for the perspective family, the range `‖p_cam‖` for a ray-path model): a length, `IN_FRONT_FLOOR_FRACTION` of the round's median in-front measure (`in_front_floor`), so that it scales with the world and rejects only a point on its camera's centre ([core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md](core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md) § "The in-front floor"). The retriangulation operation's `floor` is an angle, the narrowest ray pair a track may have before it is `thin`. Both are bounds a point is held to, on different quantities and with different consequences (an observation trimmed for a round, a track made a bearing), so neither is called the other's name, and the trim's bound is not called a *depth floor*, since for a ray-path model it bounds a range |
| **`RayBatch`** / **`TrackRays`** | `analysis::point_or_bearing::RayBatch` is many tracks of rays in the CSR layout of the batch triangulation functions (`dirs`, `centers`, `weights`, `offsets`), as `track_rays` returns it. `bench::classify::TrackRays` is the rays of one track, with their weights and the `sigma_px` they were weighted at. The bench reads both, so the batch type is named for being a batch rather than taking a second meaning of *track rays*; the function `track_rays` keeps its name, since it builds a track's rays for each track |
| **`held_point_kept`** / **`no_usable_point`** / **`bearing_behind_camera`** / **`left_unusable`** | the four outcomes of the consumer rule of the point-or-bearing test, where a verdict and what the rays can be stored as disagree: a finite verdict whose point fit gives no usable point while the place held is usable and is kept (`held_point_kept`), a finite verdict whose point fit gives no usable point and no usable place is held (`no_usable_point`), a bearing verdict whose bearing is behind an observing camera, and a track that neither describes. `InfinityReclassification` counts the last three under these names (reclassification counts a usable stored point it keeps in `kept`, since it does not refit one) and `ClassificationReason` (`HeldPointKept`, `NoUsablePoint`, `BearingBehindCamera`, `LeftUnusable`) names the bench's verdict by them, so a person reading a reclassification summary and a bench sentence reads one set of words. The verdicts themselves are named by what settled them: `score_cleared`, `midpoint_bound_cleared`, `bearing_cost_below_threshold`, `score_below_threshold` |
| **short baseline** / **single viewpoint** (a track) | a track has a short baseline when its observing cameras are too close together, across its bearing, to tell a point at the capture's own scale from infinity at the noise level: its `resolvable_distance` is under the camera extents. It names that condition, and `InfinityDiscovery::short_baseline` (the `short_baseline` key of `find_points_at_infinity`'s summary) counts the bearings discovery appends that have one; the point-or-bearing test gives such a track `Λ ≈ 0` and a bearing verdict. It was chosen over *single-stop*, which names one cause of the condition, the camera stopping to look around: a track seen from cameras spread along a walk toward the point has a short baseline as well, and a track seen from one stop can still resolve a depth when the noise is low enough. A track seen from a single viewpoint ([formats/sfmr-file-format.md](formats/sfmr-file-format.md)) is the stricter case, whose observing cameras share one optical centre, so that no noise level gives it a depth. *Indeterminate* was the third verdict of the retired inverse-depth z rule for the short-baseline geometry. No step decides on that rule now, and the point-or-bearing test has two verdicts, so the word is not used for what discovery appends or for what the bench writes |
| **SIFT index** / descriptor index | the user-facing name for the `.kdf` structure is *SIFT index*. *Descriptor index* is not a second name for it |
| **spin** / **tilt** | a spin turns the square about its own normal. A tilt turns the normal itself, is stopped `MAX_TILT_DEG` from any observation, and so reads every observation's camera. Neither moves a keypoint, since both keep the centre. In axis-angle form they look like one operation about two axes; they are two steps because a spin keeps the plane and the face and has no cap, while a tilt changes which way the surface faces |
| **fit normal** / **finite-difference normal** / **grid-plane normal** / **grown-plane normal** | the bench steps that estimate which way a track's patch faces and tilt it there, keeping its centre. `fit_normal` takes the normal the `in` sightings agree on best, by the photometric normal refinement; `finite_difference_normal` takes the plane through where smaller **pieces** of the patch fit, cut by `pieces` along each axis with neighbours overlapping by `overlap` of a piece's side (`overlap_percent` on the wire and in the viewer). Its `PieceLayout::Cross` is a row of pieces on each axis, written "3 pieces along each axis"; `PieceLayout::Grid`, the grid-plane normal, is a grid tiling the patch with a plane fitted through it, written "3x3 pieces". `grid_plane_normal` is the grid-plane normal that is to replace that layout: the plane through the nine cells of the ZNCC grid, each registered in every view, written "9 of 9 cells in the plane"; it has no pieces, and until the replacement Track View's *Grid Plane Normal* and the wire still run `PieceLayout::Grid`. `grown_plane_normal` takes one plane fitted to the photographs view by view: it is a plane, and its support grows, both the window each view is registered over, from a few samples at the keypoint to the whole grid, and the set of views the plane is combined from, one pick at a time. *Homography normal* names the warp but not that growth, and *register* is kept for giving an image a pose. Each step is named for the evidence it reads. Track View's entries are *Finite Diff Normal*, *Grid Plane Normal* and *Grown Plane Normal*, and the wire tool is `fit_bench_track_normal` with `method` `finite_difference`, `grid_plane` or `grown_plane`; `fit_normal` is a library step and a Python binding with no viewer entry. *Split* is not used for the pieces: on the bench it names splitting rows off onto a second track |
| **turned** / **undetermined** / **unstable** | the three outcomes of `grid_plane_normal` (`GridPlaneOutcome`). *Turned*: the loop converged with at least one axis fixed and the patch was turned. *Undetermined*: the cells cannot constrain the normal (no axis fixed, or no view scored), a lack of evidence. *Unstable*: they constrain it but the loop did not settle (a rejected turn, an oscillation, or the render cap without convergence). The last two leave the normal as the step was given it and are written "normal left unchanged: undetermined" or "normal left unchanged: unstable". Neither is a refusal, since the step returns its track read back, so *failed* and *refused* are not used for them; the two names keep apart the two reasons a caller acts on, missing evidence and evidence that does not settle |
| **`max_ray_angle_deg`** / **`max_angle_from_mean_deg`** | both measure how widely a track's viewing rays spread, in degrees, and they are different numbers. `max_ray_angle_deg` is the largest angle between two of the track's rays: the bench's nearby-source candidates, far-field readings and depth layers (`bench/nearby/candidate.rs`, `far_field.rs`, `layers.rs`, and `max_ray_angle` in a layer's evidence), and the viewer's `metrics::compute_max_pairwise_angle`, which the Image Detail `max_track_angle` overlay shows. `max_angle_from_mean_deg` is the largest angle of one ray from the mean viewing direction: the `PointInspection` field `analysis::inspect_point` returns and the Python `inspect_point` key of that name, printed by `sfm inspect --verbose` as *Ray spread (max angle to mean)*. The mean viewing direction is the normalised sum of the unit rays. For two rays the angle from the mean is half the pairwise angle. For more rays it is at least half of it, and while no two rays are more than 90° apart, as in an ordinary track, it is at most the pairwise angle; past 90° it can exceed it. `inspect_point` once called its value `max_ray_angle_deg` too; it was renamed because the pairwise meaning was the one the bench and the viewer already used, and one name for two numbers that differ by up to a factor of two would let a threshold written against one be read against the other. The pairwise angle keeps the shorter name, and the other says what it is measured from. Neither is called *triangulation angle* or *parallax* alone, since a reader takes either word for either angle |
| **translate** / **offset** | not a pair but a collision. `offset` names an in-plane displacement from the patch's centre of a point on its plane, such as where a pixel's ray meets it (`keypoint_plane_offset`). It does **not** name a motion of the patch, which is `translate` |

## Prose

**Avoid the em-dash, and do not swap it for ` -- `.** The em-dash is a
construction rather than a character: a parenthetical break dropped into the
middle of a sentence. Replacing the character keeps the habit and changes only
its spelling, which is how prose ends up with a dashed aside every second
paragraph in a file that never once typed an em-dash.

What the sentence wants is usually something else, and usually something
different from the last time. A comma carries a light aside. A colon introduces
the thing that explains what came before. A semicolon balances two clauses that
could each stand alone. A full stop is right when the aside was really a second
thought, and parentheses when it is genuinely an aside. Often the break was not
earning its place and the sentence reads better rewritten without it. Vary these
rather than settling on one, because a single substitution repeated is the tic
again under a new name. A real dash remains available where a sentence genuinely
calls for one; it should be occasional.

*Direction of travel:* em-dash is still the majority across `specs/` by roughly
seven to one. A convention tally on this reports the wrong majority, which is
the point of recording it here. Where an em-dash is the separator in a list
whose every sibling item uses one, match the list; the entry is about prose, not
about a file's formatting furniture.

Specs are **present tense** and describe what the code *is*. A change proposal
lives in [drafts/](drafts/README.md) and is converted before filing. That rule
and the rest of the spec contract are in [README.md](README.md), not here.
