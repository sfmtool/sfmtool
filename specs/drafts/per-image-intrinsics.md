# Per-Image Intrinsic Adjustments

**Status:** Draft. Decided:
- the bundle adjustment first releases each camera's shared principal point, with a checkbox in SfM Explorer's Bundle Adjust dialog and an argument on its MCP tool (PR 1, built; see [PR 1](#pr-1-releasing-the-shared-principal-point));
- an `.sfmr` file stores, per image, a small adjustment of its camera's focal and principal point, as one optional `images/` column per adjustable parameter, with each camera recording in `cameras/metadata.json` which parameters vary per image; this is format version 13 (PR 2, built with the accessor below and a refusal of any non-zero adjustment at the file boundary, which PR 3 lifted);
- the focal adjustment is a relative scale, `f_i = f·(1 + s_i)`, and the principal-point adjustment is stored on both axes as a fraction of the image's shorter side `m = min(width, height)`, the **short-side unit**, `cx_i = cx + a_x,i·m` and `cy_i = cy + a_y,i·m`, so that both carry over when a reconstruction moves from low-resolution proxies to the full-resolution photographs, and an offset reads the same in x and y;
- one accessor, `image_intrinsics(i)`, returns an image's camera with its adjustment applied, borrowed when there is none, and nothing else reads the raw columns (PR 2); every consumer that looks up an image's camera goes through it, and COLMAP export writes each camera that has a non-zero adjustment on any of its images as one COLMAP camera per image, with the adjustment applied (PR 3, built in three parts: PR 3a, the library and the bindings with the bundle adjustment holding the adjustments; PR 3b, the viewer; PR 3c, the Python package and the exports, with the refusal at the file boundary lifted);
- the bundle adjustment releases the adjustments per image, solving the per-image values with the camera's shared value of the same kind held, each image held toward the camera's mean adjustment by a Gaussian prior whose width is derived for each camera from the solve itself: a first pass under a loose prior that only stops runaways, a robust spread of the deviations from the mean, with the prior's pull undone and the measurement noise subtracted, weighted by how well each is determined, and further passes under that spread, which the report states; after every round the camera takes the mean and each image keeps its difference from it (PR 4);
- one camera entry per image is rejected as the way to do this.

Not decided: the floor on a derived prior width and how many derive and re-solve passes are needed, both to be checked on ordinary solves rather than the hand-curated ground truths; a focal model driven by the EXIF subject distance. See [Open questions](#open-questions).

## Purpose

A reconstruction stores a small list of cameras, and each image says which of them took it. Every image taken through one camera is then read through exactly the same lens: the same focal length, the same principal point (the pixel the optical axis passes through) and the same distortion. For a phone camera that is not quite true. Optical image stabilisation moves the lens sideways by a few pixels from one photograph to the next, which moves the principal point, and autofocus changes the focal length slightly with the focus distance ("focus breathing"). A bundle adjustment that must read every photograph through one shared lens cannot model either effect, and leaves it in the residuals as an error that grows toward the edge of the image.

This draft lets each image carry a small adjustment of its camera's focal length and principal point, stored in the `.sfmr` file beside the pose, applied wherever an image's camera is read, and solved by the bundle adjustment under a prior that keeps it small. The shared camera keeps the distortion, which is the part the images have in common and the part that needs the most data to fit. It is laid out as four pull requests, each of which leaves the tree working.

## Evidence

The measurements below were made by an experiment outside the tree, a joint bundle adjustment of poses, points and intrinsics with a Huber loss and nothing trimmed. Each is a **held-out** median reprojection error: the solve is fitted on half the points; the other half are then triangulated with the poses and intrinsics fixed, and their reprojection error is measured. A parameter that only fits noise lowers the fitted half's error and not the held-out half's.

**`dino_dog_toy`** (85 photographs, a Pixel 7 Pro main camera, 6.81 mm f/1.85 with optical image stabilisation, 2040×1536, EXIF subject distance 0.15 to 0.45 m). Its candidate ground truth has one shared `SFMTOOL_PINHOLE` camera, and its residuals grow radially toward the image edge in a way no single shared camera fits.

| Released | Held-out median |
|----------|-----------------|
| shared focal and radial distortion (baseline) | 0.818 px |
| + shared principal point, tangential, thin prism, aspect and skew | 0.757 px |
| + per-image focal | 0.732 px |
| + per-image principal point | 0.322 px |
| + per-image focal and principal point | 0.280 px |

- The per-image principal-point offsets spread over about 21 px in x and 20 px in y (1.4% and 1.3% of the short side, 1536 px), about a shared offset of roughly (−7, +19) px. They drift along the capture sequence and correlate only weakly with the direction of gravity in the camera frame, which fits the stabilised lens's position at the moment of capture.
- The per-image focal scale spreads over 1.3 to 1.6%, and correlates at −0.89 with the EXIF `SubjectDistance`: focus breathing.
- Without a prior, one image with 63 observations moved its principal point by −626 px. That is why the per-image release in PR 4 has a prior.

**`seoul_bull_sculpture`** (17 images, 270×480, `SIMPLE_RADIAL`, its checked-in ground truth) shows the same effect at a smaller size: held-out median 0.233 px at the baseline, 0.199 px with a shared principal point (a shared x offset of about −10.6 px), 0.142 px with a per-image principal point. The per-image offsets spread over about 3.2 px in x and 3.7 px in y (1.2% and 1.4% of the short side, 270 px), and the focal scale over 0.5%.

**Kerry Park** (two fisheyes on a rig) was not measured.

Both datasets measured are hand-curated ground truths, cleaner than what a solve produces: their noise level is low and their adjustments are spread less than an ordinary reconstruction's would be. The spreads above show that the effect is real and how large it is in these two, not what width a prior should have in general, which is why PR 4 derives the width from each solve instead of fixing it.

The experiment is not reproducible in the tree yet: the dino ground truth is not checked in, and of these parameters the kernel releases only the shared principal point (PR 1). PR 4's tests use synthetic scenes, and a script that repeats the held-out measurement on the checked-in ground truths is listed in [Open questions](#open-questions).

## The adjustment

An image `i` taken through camera `c` (width `w`, height `h`, short side `m = min(w, h)`, focal `f`, or `fx` and `fy` on a two-focal model, principal point `(cx, cy)`) is read through the camera's model and distortion with

```
f_i  = f  · (1 + s_i)          (fx and fy both, on a two-focal model)
cx_i = cx + a_x,i · m
cy_i = cy + a_y,i · m
```

and nothing else changed. `s_i`, `a_x,i` and `a_y,i` are the image's **intrinsic adjustment**. All three are `0` for an image whose camera does not vary them, and an image whose three are `0` is read through the shared camera exactly.

**Why a relative focal scale.** The scale is dimensionless, so it means the same at any resolution: a camera resized by a factor `k` has its focal multiplied by `k`, and `f_i` follows with `s_i` unchanged. A proxy workflow can therefore solve on low-resolution copies of the photographs, switch to the originals by resizing the shared camera, and keep every adjustment as it is. `(1 + s)` was chosen over `exp(s)` because the stored number then reads directly as "this image's focal is 1.3% longer", and because over the 1 to 2% range the evidence shows the two differ by less than `s²/2`, 0.02% at most. The one thing `exp(s)` gives that `(1 + s)` does not, a focal that cannot reach zero, is a step guard here (`s > −1`, and the image's focal above `1e-6` like the shared one). On a two-focal model the one scale moves `fx` and `fy` together, which keeps the aspect ratio the shared camera holds.

**Why a fraction of the short side, on both axes.** The **short-side unit** of an image is `m = min(w, h)` pixels, and this project uses it as the unit for a quantity measured across an image that should mean the same at any resolution and in any direction. A resize maps a pixel coordinate as `x' = x·w'/w` and `y' = y·h'/h`, exactly under the format's pixel convention (origin at the top-left corner of the image, half-pixel centres; [sfmr-file-format.md](../formats/sfmr-file-format.md) § "Pixel space"). Under a uniform resize by `k`, which is what a proxy workflow does, `m` becomes `k·m` and every offset `a·m` scales with the image, so the stored fractions carry over unchanged. A resize whose rounding changes the aspect ratio slightly (2040×1536 to 408×307, a fifth with the height rounded) scales x by 0.2 and y by 307/1536 ≈ 0.1999, and an offset in the short-side unit is then off by that difference, under 0.1% of itself, far below a pixel; fractions of the width in x and of the height in y would have been exact there. The short-side unit is chosen anyway because it is the same length in x and y: a stabilised lens that shifts sideways by some distance on the sensor moves the principal point by the same number of pixels whichever way it moves, and the stored offset says the same number on both axes, so one isotropic prior width in the stored unit is one isotropic width in pixels. It is not stored as a fraction of the focal, because the focal can itself be released, per camera and per image, and an offset that changed in pixels whenever the focal moved would couple two parameters the solve should keep apart.

**The distortion centre moves with the principal point.** In every model here the distortion is applied in normalised coordinates before the focal and principal point are, so the distortion centre is the principal point. A shift of the principal point, shared or per image, moves the distortion centre with it. That is what the evidence measures, and it is what a stabilised lens does.

### Rejected: one camera entry per image

A reconstruction can already give every image its own camera entry. That would also let each image's focal and principal point differ, but each image would then carry its own unshared copy of the distortion, and a bundle adjustment releasing the distortion would solve one spline per image, each from that image's observations alone. The distortion is the part of the lens the images do share, and the part that needs observations from many images to reach the edge of the field. The adjustment keeps it shared and lets only the three parameters that do vary move per image.

## PR 1: releasing the shared principal point

Built. The bundle adjustment releases each camera's shared principal point, per camera, on every model and with or without the focal: `CameraRelease::principal_point`, the kernel's `opt_pp`, `EditedReconstruction.bundle_adjust(opt_pp=...)` and a `"principal_point"` key in `releases`, `sfmtool.geometry.bundle_adjust(opt_pp=...)`, a **Release principal point** checkbox on each camera row of SfM Explorer's Bundle Adjust dialog, MCP `bundle_adjust`'s `release_principal_point`, and `BundleAdjustTransform.release_principal_point` on the sfmtool path. `CameraAdjustment` reports `principal_point_released`, `principal_point_before` and `principal_point_after`. The standing specs describe it: [core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md](../core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md) § "The principal-point release" (the columns, the step guard, the near-degeneracy) and § "The reduced camera system", [core/reconstruction/bundle-adjust.md](../core/reconstruction/bundle-adjust.md) (the release, the report and the write-back), [gui/edits/bundle-adjust.md](../gui/edits/bundle-adjust.md) (the checkbox, the version label and the Action Log entry) and [gui/mcp-server.md](../gui/mcp-server.md) § "`resect_camera_image` / `bundle_adjust`".

What the later PRs build on:
- **The lens block.** Each camera's block in the reduced system is `[f, k1, c₀..c_{N−1}, cx, cy]`, `4 + N_j` wide, the principal point after the spline so that `F_SLOT`, `K1_SLOT` and `BSPLINE_SLOT0` keep their values; `pp_slot(n_coeffs)` is the `cx` slot. An unreleased slot is pinned with an identity row and column and a zero gradient.
- **The per-observation block has four widths.** The principal point's two columns sit before the six pose columns, and only in the instantiations of a solve that releases some principal point: `BASE_CAM_COLS` (`2 + 6`), `BSPLINE_CAM_COLS` (`2 + BSPLINE_SUPPORT + 6`), `BASE_PP_CAM_COLS` and `BSPLINE_PP_CAM_COLS` (each two wider). This differs from the design first drafted here, which widened the two existing blocks for every solve: that made the kernel's tests about 40% slower for solves releasing no principal point, since the Schur elimination's cost grows with the square of the block's width. A solve that releases no principal point gives the same output bit for bit as before PR 1; running the kernel's and the reconstruction adjustment's test scenes (354 solves in 177 tests) before and after matched every output.
- **The step guard.** A candidate whose principal point is not finite or lies outside `[0, width] × [0, height]` is rejected whole and re-damped (`pp_step_admissible`). PR 4's per-image guard extends the same test to each image's adjusted principal point.
- **No prior on the shared principal point**, and no conditioning reported for it; the report states the shift instead. Whether it should report the conditioning is an open question of [core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md](../core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md#open-questions). The near-degeneracy that makes the per-image release need a prior is the one the kernel spec describes for the shared one: a principal-point shift and a rotation differ only by a term of order `δ·x²/f²`, plus the distortion centre's motion.
- **The CLI** gained no key: `sfm xform --bundle-adjust` holds the principal point.

## PR 2: the format columns and the accessor

PR 2 is built. It made the adjustment representable everywhere a reconstruction is held, and readable through one function, while no writer can produce a non-zero one. The standing specs describe it: [sfmr-file-format.md](../formats/sfmr-file-format.md#per-image-intrinsic-adjustments-optional-version-13) the version 13 columns `images/focal_adjustments` and `images/principal_point_adjustments_xy`, each camera's `per_image_adjustments` key, the rules a reader, a writer and a verifier hold them to, their hash slots and the migration from version 12; [edited-reconstruction.md](../core/reconstruction/edited-reconstruction.md) the in-memory form; and the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) the words *intrinsic adjustment*, *image intrinsics* and *short-side unit*.

What PRs 3 and 4 build on:
- **In memory.** Each `SfmrImage` carries `intrinsic_adjustment: IntrinsicAdjustment`, a `Copy` struct `{ focal: f64, principal_point: [f64; 2] }` whose `Default` is zero, so every edit that reorders, filters, merges or copies images carries it. The per-camera flags are `ImageSet::per_image_adjustments: Vec<PerImageAdjustments>`, one `{ focal: bool, principal_point: bool }` per camera, or empty when no camera varies anything; `ImageSet::camera_per_image_adjustments(c)` reads one camera's. A save writes a column when some camera's flag is set, and also when an image holds a non-zero value of it under a camera whose flag is not, so that the writer refuses that value rather than dropping it.
- **The accessor.** `ImageSet::image_intrinsics(i)` and `SfmrReconstruction::image_intrinsics(i)` return `Cow<CameraIntrinsics>`, borrowed when the image's adjustment is zero; `CameraIntrinsics::with_adjustment` applies one. Python has `SfmrReconstruction.image_intrinsics(i)`, and the `read_sfmr` / `write_sfmr` dicts carry the two columns and a `per_image_adjustments` list of names per camera. `ImageSet::camera_for_image` still returns the shared camera, for PR 3 to move its callers and remove.
- **The guard.** `read_sfmr` and `write_sfmr_with_options` refuse any non-zero adjustment with `SfmrError::Unsupported`, naming the first such image and saying this build does not yet read per-image intrinsic adjustments. All-zero columns and the keys pass, `verify_sfmr` does not refuse a non-zero adjustment, and `content_hash_of` and `SfmrReconstruction::content_xxh128` hash a value that holds one. A value built in memory, through `SfmrReconstruction::from_sfmr_data` or the binding's `from_data`, may hold non-zero adjustments, which is how PR 3's tests check each consumer before PR 3c lifts the guard.
- **Decided in the build.** The reader also refuses a `per_image_adjustments` list out of the order `"focal"`, `"principal_point"`, so a list has one spelling. When `clone_with_changes(cameras=…)` changes the camera count, camera `c` keeps its flags while it is still there and a new camera varies nothing.

## PR 4: releasing the adjustments per image

**The release.** `CameraRelease` gains `image_focal: bool` and `image_principal_point: bool`: whether each image of the camera has its focal scale, and its principal-point offset, solved. The kernel takes them as `opt_image_f` and `opt_image_pp` requests narrowed by `BaCameras::releases`, like the others. The per-image principal point is released on every model, for the same reason as the shared one. The per-image focal is released on the models `focal_is_releasable` admits, and `ImageFocalNotReleasable` refuses it on any other, naming the camera.

**Per-image values only.** A per-image release solves the per-image values of that parameter and nothing else of its kind: the camera's shared value of the same kind (the focal for `image_focal`, the principal point for `image_principal_point`) is held during the solve. With the shared value held and the prior centred on the camera's mean adjustment (below), the data alone decide that mean, and the prior acts only on how far each image is from it. The data depend on the shared value and an image's adjustment only through their sum, so releasing both would leave a direction the data cannot see, which only the prior could fix; holding the shared value removes that direction, and the re-centring after each round (below) moves the part every image has in common into the shared value.

**Shared and per-image of the same kind.** Asking for both the shared and the per-image release of one parameter means the same as the per-image release alone: the shared value moves only through the re-centring, and the solve is the same bit for bit. It is not an error, since a person who ticks both asks for the shared value to move as well, and it does. A per-image focal satisfies the rule that a distortion is released only with the focal, because the camera's focal moves with it, to the images' mean. The report says the shared value was released (`focal_released`, `principal_point_released`) whenever the per-image release of its kind was, beside the per-image flags, and states its change.

**The reduced camera system.** Each image's block grows by the per-image parameters. When any camera of the solve releases one, every image block is `[δθ×3, δt×3, s, a_x, a_y]`, nine wide, and the slots an image's camera does not release are pinned; when none does, it stays six wide, so a solve with no per-image release is the solve before PR 4 bit for bit. `LinState::img_slot` becomes `n_shared + 9·ci` in those solves. The per-observation camera block grows by the same three columns, after the pose, in two more instantiations, `BASE_IMG_CAM_COLS` and `BSPLINE_IMG_CAM_COLS`, which also carry the shared principal point's two columns, so that the widths stay one instantiation each and number six rather than eight. The points are still eliminated by the Schur complement; the per-image parameters couple to them through the observations, as the poses do.

The columns, at the image's adjusted camera:

```
∂(u, v)/∂s_i   = ((u − cx_i), (v − cy_i)) / (1 + s_i)     (= f·(x_d, y_d))
∂(u, v)/∂a_x,i = (m, 0)
∂(u, v)/∂a_y,i = (0, m)
```

An image of a camera that releases a per-image parameter is read through an entry of its own, whatever its adjustment, since the adjustment changes within a round. The shared focal's column stays `(u − cx_i)/f` and the `k1` and spline columns keep the image's focal `f_i`, as with held adjustments.

**The prior.** The per-image values of a camera `c` are held to the camera's mean by a Gaussian prior on each image's deviation from that mean, with widths that belong to the camera: `σ_pp,c` for the principal point, in the short-side unit and the same on both axes, so `σ_pp,c·m` pixels in x and in y; and `σ_s,c` for the focal scale, a fraction. With the data cost approximately `Σ r²` in pixels for residuals inside the loss scale, the prior enters the round's cost as

```
σ_n² · Σ_i ( ‖a_i − ā‖² / σ_pp,c²  +  (s_i − s̄)² / σ_s,c² )
```

where the sum and the means `ā` and `s̄` are over the camera's images that the round solves (the posed images with a kept observation in the round), and `σ_n` is the reprojection noise level measured at the start of the round over its kept observations (the measure `round_noise` takes after the final round; the round's loss scale where it measures nothing), fixed for the round so that the cost the damping ladder compares is one function per round, as the spline penalty's `f_ref` is. The offsets and their width are in the same unit, so `m` cancels. A camera with one image in the round has no deviation to hold, and its one image is solved by the data alone, as a shared release would be.

The prior couples the images of a camera: with `w = σ_n²/σ²` and `n` images, it adds `w·(I − 𝟏𝟏ᵀ/n)` to the camera's per-image slots of one parameter in the reduced system, the diagonal `w·(1 − 1/n)` and every pair of the camera's images `−w/n`, and `w·(a_i − ā)` to each image's gradient entry, after the data and before the damping, like the spline penalty. **It is added exactly, into the dense reduced system.** That system is already dense and factored by LU, so the coupling's `n²` entries per camera and parameter add no structure the solve has to respect and cost nothing beside the `(9·n_im)²` it already holds; the prior reads no point, so the Schur elimination is unchanged. The alternative, a camera-level mean as an extra slot with each image's prior centred on it, reaches the same minimum once that slot is eliminated, but adds slots, a mean that has to be pinned for a camera with one image, and a sparse block the dense factorization cannot exploit.

**Deriving the widths.** The widths are not fixed numbers. The ground truths the evidence came from are cleaner than an ordinary solve, so a width read off them would hold the adjustments of a typical reconstruction too tightly, and the right width differs between cameras anyway: a stabilised phone lens moves its principal point by a percent of the image, a fixed lens by nothing. So the kernel derives each camera's widths from the solve itself, an empirical Bayes estimate:

1. **A loose first pass.** The first round of the schedule runs every camera's prior at the loose widths. Their only job is to stop a runaway, an image like the dino's with 63 observations whose principal point ran to −626 px (41% of its short side) without a prior; they are wide enough that any deviation the data actually support is pulled very little.
2. **The spread, weighted by information.** After a round, for each camera with a per-image release and each released parameter, the kernel reads how well every image's adjustment is determined: its variance `v_i = σ_n² · [H_i⁻¹]_aa`, where `H_i` is that image's own block of the reduced system at the round's last linearisation, the data part only (no prior rows, no damping), with the points already eliminated. Inverting the whole block, pose and adjustment together, counts the adjustment's coupling with the same image's rotation and position against it, which is the near-degeneracy that let the runaway happen. It is the variance with every other image and the shared camera held, so it is smaller than the variance the whole reduced system would give. That is the right variance for what it is used for below: it is the same block the round's prior acts on, so it is the one that sets how far that prior pulled the image's deviation, and it ranks the images by how well each is determined. Each image weighs `1/v_i`, and an image whose block is not positive definite weighs nothing.

   The solved deviation `d_i = a_i − ā` is not the sample to measure. It was solved under the round's prior of width `σ`, which pulls it toward the mean: in the image's block the data's information on the adjustment is `σ_n²/v_i` in the cost's units and the prior's is about `σ_n²/σ²`, so `d_i ≈ d̂_i · σ²/(σ² + v_i)`, where `d̂_i` is the deviation the data alone give. A spread of the `d_i` would be low by that factor, and a width derived from it and solved under again would shrink the next spread further, running the width down to the floor whenever the images' `v_i` are comparable to the true spread, which is the ordinary solve. So the kernel first undoes the pull, image by image:

   ```
   d̂_i = d_i · (1 + v_i / σ²)
   ```

   with `σ` the width the round ran under and `v_i` the same data-only variance as the weight. For the principal point the two axes are undone together, `d̂_i = (I + V_i/σ²) d_i` with `V_i = σ_n² · [H_i⁻¹]_aa` the 2×2 block of both axes, whose diagonal entries are the axes' `v_i`. With the focal and the principal point both released, all three are undone together in the same way, `d̂_i = (I + V_i P⁻¹) d_i` with `V_i` the 3×3 block of the adjustments and `P = diag(σ_s², σ_pp², σ_pp²)` the round's prior variances, since the same image's focal scale and principal point can be correlated. The prior's own diagonal is `w·(1 − 1/n)` rather than `w`; the factor is left out, which makes `d̂_i` slightly small for a camera of few images, and the spread with it.

   `d̂_i` is then the true deviation plus measurement noise of variance `v_i`, so its spread overstates the camera's by that noise. The kernel removes it, again with medians:

   ```
   m  = 1.4826 · median_w |d̂_i|
   s² = max(0, m² − median_w v_i)
   ```

   where `median_w` is the median weighted by `1/v_i` over the camera's images the round solved, and `s` is the camera's spread. For the principal point the x and y deviations are pooled as two samples per image, each with its own axis's `v_i` and weight, which the short-side unit makes comparable. If the adjustments are Gaussian about the camera's mean with spread `τ` and the well-weighted images have similar `v_i`, then `d̂_i` has variance `τ² + v_i`, so `m²` estimates `τ² + v` and subtracting the typical `v` leaves `τ²`. Both terms are medians, so one wild image counts as one sample whatever its value, and the weight makes an image with few observations count for almost nothing in either; it still moves the mean `ā` by its share, as it moves the prior's centre. The factor `1.4826` makes the median absolute value of a zero-mean Gaussian sample its standard deviation. The spread is measured about the mean because the prior is centred there: an offset every image shares is the shared value's, which the re-centring moves into the camera, and is not a spread. Like the weight, the undoing is exact only for the image's own block with every other image and the shared camera held; that is accurate enough here because an image's adjustment couples to the others only through the shared points and the prior. What remains errs high, not low: `d̂_i` carries the error of the other images' estimates as well as its own, so its noise variance is larger than the conditional `v_i`, the subtraction removes slightly too little, and `s` comes out slightly above the true spread, which leaves the prior slightly loose rather than pinning the adjustments.
3. **The width.** The camera's width for the next round is `s` clamped between the floor and the loose width. The floor keeps a camera whose images do not vary from being pinned to exactly its mean by a spread that rounds to nothing; the loose width as a cap keeps a derived width from releasing more than the first pass did. A camera with fewer than two images of non-zero weight keeps the loose width, since one image has no spread, and reports no spread for that parameter.
4. **The report.** Each camera's derived widths, and the spread they came from before clamping, go into the report (below).

**Where the derivation runs in the schedule.** The kernel derives the widths again before every round after the first, from the round just finished, and the round runs under them. The rounds are where the cost is already fixed and re-measured, `σ_n` included, so changing the widths between rounds keeps each round's cost one function, as the damping ladder needs; changing them inside a round would not. And re-deriving each time follows the trim: the first round, at a 50 px trim and a 5 px loss scale, still holds gross outliers that spread the adjustments wider than the clean observations do, so the final round runs under a width measured on the set the 12 px round left. The previous width enters a derivation only through the undoing of its pull, which removes it, so deriving again on the same observations gives the same width up to the change in linearisation: re-deriving follows the trim and does not ratchet the width down. With `DEFAULT_SCHEDULE`'s three rounds that is two derivations, the final round under widths from the second round's solution. A schedule of one round runs that round a second time under widths derived from the first, so that every solve with a per-image release derives at least once; with `fixed` widths it runs once.

**Re-centring after every round.** After each round, for each camera with a per-image release, the camera takes the mean of the released adjustments over its images the round solved, and every image of the camera keeps its difference from that mean:

- principal point: `cx += mean(a_x)·m`, `cy += mean(a_y)·m`, then `a_i −= mean(a)` on each axis;
- focal: `f ← f·mean(1 + s_i)` (every focal of a two-focal model), then `1 + s_i ← (1 + s_i)/mean(1 + s_i)`, so that the mean focal scale is exactly zero.

The re-centring is applied to every image of the camera, the ones the round did not solve and the unposed ones outside the kernel included (the reconstruction-level adjustment applies the same shift to its unposed images), so every image's absolute intrinsics are unchanged by it, to rounding. The mean is over the images the round solved, the same set the prior's mean is over, because those are the images whose adjustments the round's data fixed; an image no round reached keeps whatever adjustment it had relative to the camera's new value. It runs after every round rather than once at the end, so that the shared value is the images' mean at the start of every round: the spline penalty's `f_ref`, the `k1` guard's imaged field and the shared principal point's guard read the shared value, and each reads it as the camera's typical image only when the adjustments are centred on it. The prior and the derivation are unchanged by the re-centring, since both measure about the mean.

**The options.** The settings are the loose widths, the floor and an override, `BundleAdjustOptions::image_prior: ImagePriorOptions`, which the kernel takes as `image_prior: Option<&ImagePriorOptions>` (`None` for the default):

```rust
/// A prior's widths for one camera: the focal scale's as a fraction, the
/// principal point's in the short-side unit.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct ImagePriorWidths {
    pub focal: f64,
    pub principal_point: f64,
}

pub struct ImagePriorOptions {
    /// The widths of the first round, and the most a derived width may be.
    pub loose: ImagePriorWidths,
    /// The least a derived width may be.
    pub floor: ImagePriorWidths,
    /// When set, every camera's prior has these widths in every round and
    /// nothing is derived.
    pub fixed: Option<ImagePriorWidths>,
}
```

`Default` gives `loose` `0.1` for both (154 px on the dino's short side of 1536, seven to eight times the spreads either ground truth shows, and the runaway at four widths), `floor` `0.001` for both, provisionally (see [Open questions](#open-questions)), and `fixed` `None`. `fixed` is for a caller that knows the widths, such as a test or a camera measured before; the widths it gives are used as they are, without the floor or the cap.

The prior is what makes the per-image release well posed. For one image a principal-point shift and a rotation of that image alone differ only by the field-curvature term of the shared release ([core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md](../core/geometry/bundle-adjustment.md) § "The principal-point release") and the distortion centre's motion, and a focal scale and a move of that camera along its optical axis differ only by the scene's depth variation; with few observations either difference is below the noise.

**Step guards.** A candidate step is rejected and re-damped when an image's focal scale is not finite or `1 + s_i ≤ 0`, its focal `f·(1 + s_i)` is not finite and above `1e-6`, or its adjusted principal point is not finite or leaves the image (`cx + a_x,i·m` outside `[0, width]` or `cy + a_y,i·m` outside `[0, height]`). The principal point's test reads every image of the round whose camera releases its principal point, shared or per image, and whose adjustment is released or not zero; an image with a zero held adjustment is the shared principal point, which its own guard already checks.

**Write-back and the report.** The kernel's `BundleAdjustment` returns `image_adjustments`, every image's adjustment after the solve, and `image_priors`, one `Option<ImagePrior>` per camera, `Some` for a camera with a per-image release: `widths`, the widths the final round ran under, `derived`, false when `fixed` set them, and `spread`, the last derivation's weighted spread before it was clamped (`NaN` for a parameter not released per image or with fewer than two weighted images, and both `NaN` when nothing was derived). In the reconstruction-level adjustment, each posed image of a camera with a per-image release takes its solved adjustment, and the camera's `per_image_adjustments` gains the released parameters, so that a solved zero is recorded as solved. An unposed image of such a camera takes the re-centring's shift, so it keeps its absolute intrinsics. The camera takes the kernel's camera, with its re-centred shared value. Each `CameraAdjustment` gains `image_focal_released` and `image_principal_point_released`, and `image_adjustments: Option<ImageAdjustmentSpread>`, present when the camera released a per-image parameter: the number of posed images, the mean and standard deviation of their focal scales (as fractions) and of their principal-point offsets (in pixels per axis), which after the re-centring have a mean of zero; the prior, `prior: ImagePriorWidths`, with `prior_derived: bool` and `spread: ImagePriorWidths`, the kernel's, so a person sees whether the floor or the cap decided the width; and `near_prior: Vec<NearPrior>`, the images whose adjustment ended more than two prior widths from the mean, each with its image index, the parameter and its distance in widths (the focal scale's `|s_i − s̄|/σ_s`, the principal point's `‖a_i − ā‖/σ_pp`). The derived widths are the solve's own estimate of how far this camera's lens moves between photographs, so they are an output worth reading in their own right. An adjustment more than two widths out is one the data pulled well past what the rest of the camera's images show, so these are the images a person should look at. The version label names the per-image releases (`, focal and principal point released per image`), and the Action Log entry adds a clause per such camera naming its widths, whether they were derived, and how many images ended beyond two of them, beside the shared value's change.

**The surfaces.** Python: `opt_image_f=False` and `opt_image_pp=False` on `EditedReconstruction.bundle_adjust` and on the kernel binding, `"image_focal"` and `"image_principal_point"` keys in `releases`, an `image_prior` keyword taking a dict with the keys `loose`, `floor` and `fixed`, each a dict with `focal` and `principal_point` (`fixed` may be `None`), and the spread and widths in the report; the kernel binding takes and returns the `(N, 3)` adjustments `[focal, a_x, a_y]` and returns each camera's widths. MCP: `release_image_focal` and `release_image_principal_point`, defaults and per-camera fields, and an `image_prior` argument of the same shape as Python's; the reply carries each camera's spread and widths. The dialog: **Release focal length per image** and **Release principal point per image** on each camera row, clear by default; ticking one ticks and greys the shared box of the same kind, whose hover text says that the per-image release moves the camera's value to the images' mean; the per-image focal is greyed where the shared focal is. `sfm xform --bundle-adjust` gains no key in PR 4.

**Tests.** On a synthetic scene with planted per-image principal-point offsets of about 1% of the short side and focal scales of about 1% about a non-zero common part, the released solve recovering each within a tolerance set by the observation count, the shared value ending at the planted mean, and the camera's adjustments ending with a mean of zero to rounding, for the principal point and the focal scale. On a synthetic scene whose adjustments are drawn with a known spread, the derived width recovering that spread within a tolerance set by the number of images; a scene whose images have few enough observations that each image's `v_i` is comparable to the planted spread's square, where the derived width recovers the planted spread within a tolerance and does not fall over five successive rounds on the same observations, which checks both the undoing of the prior's pull and the noise subtraction; the first scene with one added image of few observations and a large planted adjustment deriving nearly the same width, so that an image with little information does not inflate it; a camera with no planted variation deriving the floor; `fixed` used as given, with nothing derived; a one-round schedule deriving once; an image outside the solve keeping its absolute intrinsics through the re-centring; the shared and per-image release of one kind giving the per-image release's solve bit for bit; the column checks against central differences; the guards; parity with no per-image release; the write-back setting the camera's flags and shifting an unposed image; the spread, the widths and the near-prior list in the report; and the Python, MCP and dialog surfaces.

## Open questions

- **The derivation's floor and passes.** The floor of `0.001` is provisional, and so is whether the derivation between rounds is enough or the final round should run once more under widths derived from its own solution. Both are to be checked on solves noisier than the hand-curated ground truths, which understate an ordinary solve's noise and spread: Kerry Park (whose two fisheyes are the first case with a rig and a wide field) and the raw output of `sfm solve` on `dino_dog_toy` and `seoul_bull_sculpture` among them, with a script under `scripts/` that repeats the held-out split and compares the derived widths with the held-out error at fixed widths around them.
- **The CLI.** Whether `sfm xform --bundle-adjust` gains keys for the principal point and the per-image releases.
- **Two-focal models.** The focal-scale column `(u − cx_i, v − cy_i)/(1 + s_i)` is exact on a two-focal model as well, since one scale moves both focals. Whether PR 4 widens the per-image focal release past `focal_is_releasable` is open.
